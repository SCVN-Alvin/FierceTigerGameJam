WHO THIS IS FOR
    The AI or person producing map JSON, and whoever tunes the engine. Section 1
    is the ground truth about what "coating" actually is - read it before acting
    on sections 2-3, because the feature does not work the way the name suggests.

DECISION ON RECORD
    OPTION A is the priority. OPTION B is the fallback, to be used only if A
    cannot be delivered. Both are written out in full in section 3 and 4 so the
    choice can be made without going back to the conversation.


================================================================================
1. WHAT "COATING" IS - GROUND TRUTH
================================================================================

1.1  IT IS NOT A MATERIAL, A MESH, OR AN EXTRA SURFACE LAYER
    There is a Wall Panel mesh (FBX/Brick_Wall.fbx, FBX/Concrete_Wall.fbx) that
    the engine stretches across an intact wall instead of welding cubes. It is
    PURELY COSMETIC. KnockdownLayoutMapAuthoring.ResolveWallDurability() computes
    hit points from the blocks, and it runs whichever mesh path was taken. The
    panel adds no hit points, no thickness, no extra shot.
    Do not design difficulty around the panel. It is a rendering detail.

1.2  THE REAL MECHANIC IS THE WALL ITSELF
    A group of cells sharing a wall_id becomes ONE rigidbody whose hit points are
    the sum of its blocks. Two consequences, and the second is the one that
    matters here:

      a) Pooled hit points. A 20-cell brick wall is 60 HP in one body; the same
         20 cells loose are twenty separate 3 HP targets.
      b) A SEPARATE DAMAGE NUMBER. BulletDefinition.MaterialDamage carries both
         blockDamage and wallDamage per material per bullet level. The engine
         picks by target: a wall takes wallDamage, a loose block takes
         blockDamage. The tooltip in BulletDefinition.cs states the intent
         exactly: "Lower than blockDamage is what makes a shot chip a wall while
         destroying a lone block."

    So: CELLS IN A NAMED WALL = COATED.  LOOSE CELLS = UNCOATED.

1.3  THE NUMBERS AS SHIPPED (Assets/GameJam/Config/Bullets/)

        ammo          material    blockDamage   wallDamage   shell factor
        Rock  Lv1     glass            5            5          none
                      brick            3            1          3.0x
                      concrete         0            0          impossible
        Rock  Lv2     glass            6            6          none
                      brick            4            3          1.3x
                      concrete         0            0          impossible
        Cannon Lv1    glass            8            8          none
                      brick            6            5          1.2x
                      concrete         4            2          2.0x
        Cannon Lv2    glass           10           10          none
                      brick            8            7          1.1x
                      concrete         7            5          1.4x

1.4  THREE FACTS THAT CONSTRAIN EVERY DESIGN BELOW
    - GLASS HAS NO SHELL AT ALL. wallDamage == blockDamage at every level of
      every ammunition. Coating glass changes difficulty by exactly zero.
      Grouping glass into walls is still worth doing, but as an ASMR knob: a
      glass wall releases every pane in one second instead of popping one by one.
    - CONCRETE IS IMMUNE TO ROCK. Zero, not slow. Any level whose clear condition
      depends on breaking concrete is gated behind owning the Cannon.
    - THE SHELL IS STRONGEST EARLY AND FADES. Rock Lv1 vs brick is a 3x shell;
      by Cannon Lv2 it is 1.1x. Coating is therefore a much bigger difficulty
      lever for a player on starter gear than for an upgraded one.


================================================================================
2. THE OBSTACLE - WHY A LITERAL READING OF OPTION A BREAKS
================================================================================

"Uncoated" means loose cells, and every loose cell is its own rigidbody on the
opening frame. The budget in the spec is 150 loose bodies; beyond that Android
frame time collapses. Measured against the maps as they stand today:

        LV  map                              entries  brick  glass  concrete
         1  map_001_hollow_brick_hut              77     96      -        -
         2  map_002_brick_courtyard_tower        104    126      -        -
         3  map_003_three_storey_brick_house     217    283      -        -
         4  map_004_glass_belt_warehouse         166    138     60        -
         5  map_005_two_storey_glass_house       184    204     26        -
         6  map_006_large_glass_school           222    222     48        -
         7  map_007_concrete_frame_school        220    138     28       84
         8  test_03 (Village Gatehouse)          354     92     56      216
         9  map_009_hollow_concrete_tower        268    218     18       76
        (brick/glass/concrete columns are CELL counts, not entries)

Mission 1 under Option A wants brick uncoated on LV1-6. Loose body count that
would produce: LV1 96, LV2 126, LV3 283, LV4 198, LV5 230, LV6 270. Everything
from LV3 up blows the 150 cap, some by nearly double. The same problem hits
Mission 1 LV8, whose 216 concrete cells would all be loose.

This is not a reason to abandon Option A. It is a reason to implement it the way
section 5 describes rather than by literally un-grouping cells.


================================================================================
3. OPTION A - THE PRIORITY DESIGN
================================================================================

Read "coating on material M" as: cells of material M take wallDamage instead of
blockDamage, i.e. they behave as an armoured shell until the shell is broken.

    MISSION 1 - residential / school
        LV 1-6   no coating on anything. Brick comes off block by block; this is
                 where the player learns that bricks are individually cheap.
        LV 7-9   BRICK COATED. First contact with a shell. On Rock Lv1 this is a
                 3x jump, which is exactly the intended difficulty step.
                 Concrete (LV7-9) stays uncoated.
        Glass    never coated anywhere in Mission 1 (no effect anyway); still
                 grouped into walls per pane group for the shatter moment.

    MISSION 2 - beach architecture
        LV 1-9   BRICK COATED throughout. The brick shell is now the baseline.
        LV 7-9   CONCRETE COATED as well. Second shell introduced, and on Cannon
                 Lv1 concrete is a 2x shell - the hardest single step in the
                 campaign so far.
        Glass    grouped for shatter, never counted as coating.

    MISSION 3 - space / spaceport
        LV 1-9   BOTH BRICK AND CONCRETE COATED from level 1. No ramp-in; the
                 player is expected to arrive with upgraded ammunition.
        LV 9     BOSS. Designed to be very hard to solve, not merely tanky. See
                 the design note below - raw hit points is the wrong way to do
                 this.

    THE SHAPE OF THE RAMP, STATED PLAINLY
        M1 LV1-6   0 shells
        M1 LV7-9   1 shell   (brick)
        M2 LV1-6   1 shell   (brick)
        M2 LV7-9   2 shells  (brick + concrete)
        M3 LV1-9   2 shells  (brick + concrete)

    LEVEL 9 OF MISSION 3 - HOW TO MAKE IT HARD HONESTLY
        Do not reach for more hit points. A wall of 400 concrete cells is not
        difficult, it is long. Difficulty comes from the shot economy:
          - one load-bearing element that must be found (a hidden pillar behind
            a glass belt, reachable only through a window)
          - a collapse that only completes if the order is right (fell the
            skybridge before the tower it leans on, and the tower survives)
          - a concrete core that cannot be shot directly, only crushed by
            dropping something heavy on it
          - ammunition budget tuned so a wasted shot is felt
        Sections S1-S5 of the Blueprint document cover these patterns.


================================================================================
4. OPTION B - THE FALLBACK
================================================================================

Leave the current behaviour exactly as it is: every structural cell is grouped
into a named wall by the face/storey algorithm, so everything is effectively
coated everywhere, and difficulty comes only from entry count, material mix and
ammunition budget.

Use this only if section 5's implementation cannot be delivered. It is not
wrong - it is simply the state the campaign already ships in, and it wastes the
coating as a progression lever.


================================================================================
5. HOW TO IMPLEMENT OPTION A - RECOMMENDED
================================================================================

5.1  THE PROBLEM IN ONE SENTENCE
    Coating is currently welded to grouping: to remove a shell you must un-group
    cells, and un-grouping costs rigidbodies the platform cannot pay for.

5.2  THE FIX - DECOUPLE THEM WITH ONE FLAG
    Let a wall stay a wall for physics and rendering, but carry a flag that says
    whether it is armoured. Unarmoured walls take blockDamage.

    JSON - the flag is an OPT-OUT, absent means armoured:
        "wall": { "wall_id": "front_brick_b0_3", "bare": true }

    It is written as an opt-out rather than "armored": false on purpose.
    JsonUtility gives a field the JSON does not mention the type default, and
    for bool that is false; an "armored" field would have depended on field
    initialisers running and would have silently disarmed every existing wall if
    that assumption were ever wrong. "bare" cannot fail that way.

    IMPLEMENTED 2026-08-30 - four edits, already in the branch:
        1. KnockdownMapDefinition.cs      - KnockdownMapWallRef.bare, plus
                                            KnockdownMapBlock.WallArmored.
        2. KnockdownLayoutMapAuthoring.cs - WallBuild.Armored, set in
                                            CreateWallBuild as the AND over the
                                            group's blocks, passed to Initialize.
        3. BreakableWall.cs               - serialized "armored" field (so a
                                            baked map prefab keeps it), IsArmored,
                                            Initialize(..., bool isArmored = true).
        4. GridKnockdownCannonProjectile.cs - ResolveDamage is now called with
                                            (wall != null && wall.IsArmored), so
                                            a bare wall returns blockDamage. No
                                            new damage maths.

    What this buys:
        - Option A becomes exact, per material, per level, with no compromise.
        - Loose body count never rises; walls stay grouped, frame time unchanged.
        - Every existing map keeps behaving identically (absent flag = armoured).
        - The knob is per wall, not per map, so a single building can mix an
          armoured concrete frame with unarmoured brick infill - which is the
          A7 exoskeleton pattern from the Handbook, finally readable in play.

5.3  AUTHORING RULE ONCE THE FLAG EXISTS
    Set "bare": true on every wall whose material is uncoated at that level per
    section 3. Leave it out otherwise. Nothing else about wall grouping,
    sizes, footing or brick_2x1 merging changes - keep following the Handbook.


================================================================================
6. FALLBACK IMPLEMENTATION - IF THE ENGINE CANNOT CHANGE
================================================================================

If the flag in section 5 is not available, approximate Option A with grouping,
under a hard rule so the result stays shippable:

    R1  LOOSE CAP IS ABSOLUTE. Never exceed 150 loose bodies on the opening
        frame, whatever the design says. A level that stutters is worse than a
        level with the wrong shell.
    R2  WHEN UNCOATED CELLS EXCEED THE CAP, WALL THE STRUCTURE, LOOSEN THE SKIN.
        Keep in walls: foundations (row 0), corner piers, lintel bands, roof and
        floor slabs - the cells that hold the building up. Leave loose: the
        outer face panels between them. The player then experiences shooting
        bricks off one at a time, which is the feel of "no coating", while the
        skeleton stays cheap and the building still collapses properly.
    R3  NEVER LEAVE A BODY UNFOOTED. The footing repair pass still applies; a
        loosened skin cell that ends up floating hangs forever and blocks the
        100% clear.
    R4  SHRINK BEFORE YOU BREAK THE CAP. A level that cannot be authored
        uncoated within 150 loose bodies should lose cells - more openings,
        hollower core - rather than gain walls. Holes are free budget.
    R5  ACCEPT THE APPROXIMATION AND WRITE IT DOWN. Deliver the manifest line
        with a "coating: partial (n% loose)" note so the tuning pass knows what
        it is looking at.
