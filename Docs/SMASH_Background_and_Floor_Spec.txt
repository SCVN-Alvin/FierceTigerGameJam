================================================================================
SMASH - BACKGROUND AND FLOOR SPEC
What the camera actually shows, and the values art has to hit
Project: Demolition Contracts (FierceTigerGameJam)     Revision 1 - 2026-08-31
================================================================================

WHY THIS EXISTS
    Three background paintings in a row were composed for the whole canvas and
    then lost most of their subject when the game cropped them. Two of the three
    put their main elements exactly where the camera cannot see. Every number
    below was measured out of the scene and the shipped art, not estimated, and
    it is here rather than in a chat log because the same mistake has now been
    made three times.


================================================================================
1. THE SAFE BOX - THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT NUMBER
================================================================================

A backdrop sprite is 20 x 16.7 world units. The camera is a perspective camera
with a 22.5 degree VERTICAL field of view, sitting at y 1.01, z -8.74, pitched
1.282 degrees down. The backdrop plane is at z 19.43, so the camera is 28.17
units from it.

    visible height = 2 x 28.17 x tan(11.25 deg)      = 11.21 units of 16.7
    visible width  = 11.21 x 9/16 (portrait 9:16)    =  6.30 units of 20

Which lands as:

    HORIZONTAL      34% to 66% of the image width
    VERTICAL        28% to 95% of the image height, measured from the TOP

    On a 2000 x 1670 canvas that is a box of 628 x 1122 pixels in the middle.

    The outer two thirds of the width are NEVER on screen.
    The top quarter is NEVER on screen.

CHECKED AGAINST THE SHIPPED ART
    BG_Beach_01 has its sea horizon at 47% of the image height. The box predicts
    it appears 28% down the screen, and that is where it is in play.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR COMPOSITION
    Anything that must be seen goes inside the box. Anything outside it is a
    continuation that keeps the picture whole for the artist and is thrown away
    by the game.

    Framing elements - rocks, walls, cliffs, hangar sides - do NOT go at the
    edges of the canvas. They go at roughly 28-40% and 60-72% of the width, so
    each leans into the visible middle from its own side. At the canvas edge
    they are invisible; dead centre they collide with the gameplay.

    A worked failure, measured: the beach painting of 2026-08-31 put its two
    rock-and-palm clusters at 20-36% and 67-88%. The left one showed a 2% sliver
    and the right one did not appear at all.

IF THE CAMERA CHANGES, RECOMPUTE
    All of the above comes from four numbers in Scene/Gameplay.unity: the
    camera's field of view, its position, its pitch, and the backdrop plane's z.
    Change any of them and this section is wrong.


================================================================================
2. VALUES - WHAT THE FLOOR HAS TO DO
================================================================================

The blocks the player shoots, measured from their albedo textures:

    concrete   #70726E   luminance 113
    brick      #AB5C3A   luminance 112
    glass      #B8DBEB   luminance 214

Brick and concrete are the SAME luminance. They separate by hue only, which is
enough against each other and useless against a floor. So:

    THE FLOOR MUST BE FAR FROM 112, AND THE FAR SIDE HAS TO BE LIGHTER.

    beach, shipping and correct   sand 237, blocks 112, separation 125
    space deck, first attempt     deck 103, blocks 112, separation  -9
    space deck, corrected         deck 180, blocks 112, separation  68

A dark floor is the natural instinct for a night or space scene and it is the
one thing that cannot be done: grey concrete blocks dissolve into it. Keep the
floor at 70 percent of white or above and let the SKY carry the darkness - the
sky is above everything and nothing has to be read against it.

Sky may be as dark as 12-25 percent. Mid-distance structures sit around 45. The
floor is always the brightest thing in the frame.


================================================================================
3. THE TWO FILES, AND HOW THEY MEET
================================================================================

BACKGROUND SPRITE
    2000 x 1670 pixels exactly, aspect 1.198.
    Texture Type must be Sprite (2D and UI) or the picker will not find it.
    Name must begin with BG.

    LevelScenery rescales any sprite to cover the same world area, so a picture
    of a different size still fills the frame - but a different ASPECT is
    stretched. The beach painting of 2026-08-31 came in at 1.333 and would have
    been pulled 11 percent taller.

FLOOR TEXTURE
    Square, tileable, name begins with Floor. It is a Texture, not a Material
    and not a Sprite: the ground keeps one material for the whole game and only
    the picture on it changes.

    TILING travels with the picture, per level, set in the Mission Editor beside
    the floor slot. The ground plane is forty metres across, so:

        grass, sand, anything without straight lines      1
        deck plating, tiles, anything with a grid         8

    At 1 a deck plate is stretched to the width of a building. Grass hides that;
    plating does not.

    NO LANDMARKS. A tile can be mathematically seamless and still read as an
    obvious grid if it contains one thing the eye can pick out. A single hazard
    stripe did exactly that: the tile's edges matched perfectly and the floor
    still looked like wallpaper, because the stripe repeated every 5 metres.
    No stripes, arrows, numbers, hatches, drains or logos.

    SEAM TEST - run it, do not eyeball it:
        compare the average pixel difference between the left and right edge
        columns against the average difference between neighbouring columns
        inside the image. Under about 2x is seamless.
            space deck plate   3.08 vs 2.82   ratio 1.1   good
            beach sand         3.75 vs 1.75   ratio 2.1   marginal

WHERE THEY MEET
    The background paints its own ground in the lower part of the frame, and the
    3D floor plane is drawn over it. Those two must be the same tone or the join
    shows as a band. Beach: painted sand 237, tile 240 - invisible. Aim within
    about 10 levels of luminance.


================================================================================
4. BRIEF TEMPLATE
================================================================================

Give the artist these, filled in, every time:

    canvas            2000 x 1670 exactly
    safe box          middle third of the width, 28-95 percent of the height
    framing elements  at 28-40 and 60-72 percent of the width
    horizon           at N percent of the image height
    floor luminance   about X percent of white, and the tile it must match
    sky luminance     about Y percent of white
    forbidden         foreground objects, anything standing on the play area,
                      cables across the frame, lens flare, god rays, vignette,
                      text, characters

And read back the three measurements from the delivered file before accepting
it: canvas aspect, floor luminance, and where the main elements fall as a
percentage of the width.


================================================================================
5. THE THREE FAILURES, SO THEY ARE NOT REPEATED
================================================================================

    TOO DARK        space background at luminance 28 against blocks at 112.
                    Concrete vanished into the deck. Fixed by lifting the floor,
                    not by lifting the sky.

    TOO PALE        the correction overshot to 218 and read as a pastel sunrise.
                    "Brighter" is a value instruction and needs a number, not an
                    adjective.

    WRONG PLACE     walls, then rocks, composed at the canvas edges and cropped
                    away entirely. This is the one that keeps happening, and
                    section 1 is the answer to it.
