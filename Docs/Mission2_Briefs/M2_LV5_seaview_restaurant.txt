THE PITCH
    A two-storey block: solid brick ground floor, an entire storey of glass above
    it as the panorama dining room, and a brick roof terrace on top. The glass
    waist - the building visibly sits on its own weakest floor.

HARD CONSTRAINTS
    map id        m2_05_seaview_restaurant
    grid          12 wide x 9 tall x 7 layers
    entries       ~200
    materials     brick + glass
    band          mid (120-220)
    loose bodies  <= 40

THE SUPPORT TARGET   <-- the reason this level exists
    The glass storey itself. Break the dining room and the whole roof terrace drops
    straight through it onto the ground floor. Glass is 1 HP; this is the cheapest
    big collapse in the mission and the player should feel clever for seeing it.

BUILD RECIPE
    - Ground floor: rows y0-y3, full perimeter x0-x11 by z0-z6, one cell thick,
      hollow, with a wide entrance 3 wide x 3 tall.
    - Glass storey: rows y4-y6, THE ENTIRE PERIMETER IN GLASS. No brick corner
      piers, no brick mullions. Group it as 4-6 walls (one per face, split by
      size) so a hit anywhere in a face releases that whole face.
    - Roof terrace: rows y7-y8, brick, with a low parapet and loose parasol stubs
      on top. Heavy enough to fall convincingly.
    - Interior: one row of floor slab at y4 inside the glass storey, so the drop
      pancakes in two stages instead of one.
    - Loose: 6-10 parasol stubs and table blocks on the terrace.

COATING FLAGS
    brick    armoured - leave the flag out entirely
    glass    "bare": true on every glass wall

PANEL / WELD INTENT
    The glass faces are single-layer rectangles and render smooth - that is the
    right look for a panorama window, keep it.
    The brick floors weld. Nothing to force.

WATCH OUT
    - DO NOT put a brick pier through the glass storey to "hold the roof up". The
      roof being held only by glass IS the level. The engine is fine with it: the
      roof is footed on the glass, and when the glass goes the roof is released
      because the glass sat directly under it.
    - Keep the glass storey exactly three rows. Two reads as a window band, four
      makes the building look like an aquarium.
    - Watch the entry count: a full-perimeter glass storey is 3 rows x ~34
      perimeter cells = ~100 cells on its own. Hollow the ground floor hard.

IMAGE PROMPT (render at 1 brick = 1 cell of the grid above)
    "isometric voxel seaside restaurant, solid sandy brick ground floor, the
    entire middle storey made of pale blue glass with no columns, brick rooftop
    terrace with small blocks and parasols on it, chunky cube blocks, plain white
    background"
