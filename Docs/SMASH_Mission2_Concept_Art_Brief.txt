SMASH - MISSION 2 - CONCEPT ART BRIEF
Nine beach buildings, drawn so they can actually be built
For the AI producing concept images. Self-contained - you need no other document.
Project: Demolition Contracts (FierceTigerGameJam)     Revision 1 - 2026-08-30
================================================================================

YOUR JOB
    Draw nine buildings. One image per level, to the cell dimensions given in
    section 5. That is the whole deliverable.

NOT YOUR JOB
    The map JSON. Someone else writes it from your image. Do not produce JSON,
    wall groupings, manifests or entry counts - they will be discarded.

WHY THE SPLIT
    Your images are good. The two JSONs delivered for Level 2 were not: one had
    the wrong id, the other had a stair six cells deep when the order said one.
    Meanwhile the concept image you drew for that level was better than either
    render. Play to that. Draw; the geometry is handled downstream.


================================================================================
1. THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST
================================================================================

    ONE BRICK IN YOUR IMAGE = ONE CELL OF THE GRID.

Every building is made of cubes 0.25 m on a side. There are no smaller pieces,
ever. If you draw a wall as a hundred little bricks and the grid says the wall
is six cells wide, you have promised something the game cannot build, and the
image gets rejected. This has already happened once.

    Before drawing, read the grid line for that level: "12 wide x 12 tall x 6
    deep" means exactly 12 cubes across, 12 up, 6 back. Count them in the image.

    A useful self-check: can you count the cubes along the top edge and get the
    stated width? If not, redraw.


================================================================================
2. THE PALETTE - THREE MATERIALS, NOTHING ELSE
================================================================================

    BRICK      warm sandy red-brown. Sand-brick, bleached timber, decking.
               The body of almost everything in this mission.
    GLASS      pale blue, slightly transparent. Sea-view glazing, wind screens,
               slide tubes, lantern rooms.
    CONCRETE   flat mid grey. Stone piers, breakwater, rock plinths, the
               lighthouse base. Appears from Level 7 only.

    No wood grain, no thatch, no fabric, no metal, no water, no sand texture.
    The beach is carried by SHAPE, not by inventing materials.

    Background: plain white or very light grey. No ground plane, no sky, no
    scenery. The building alone.

    View: isometric, three-quarter, slightly from above - the same angle for all
    nine, so the set reads as one family.


================================================================================
3. WHAT MAKES A DRAWING BUILDABLE
================================================================================

3.1  EVERYTHING RAISED STANDS ON A VISIBLE LEG
    Beach buildings sit on stilts, piles and posts, and that is the point of the
    whole mission - the player wins by cutting the support, not by grinding the
    mass. So every raised thing in your image must be VISIBLY carried:

        a hut on stilts    -> draw the stilts, and draw the floor resting on
                              their tops, not floating a cell above them
        a pier deck        -> draw a post under the deck at every bent
        a cantilever, a
        slide, a balcony   -> draw a prop under it every few cells

    Anything that appears to hang off the side of a building with nothing
    underneath cannot be built: in the engine it would hang in the air forever
    and make the level impossible to finish. If you draw it, it gets cut.

3.2  HOLLOW, NOT SOLID
    These are shells one cell thick with real openings. Draw doors and windows
    as actual holes you can see through, not as painted rectangles.

3.3  OPENINGS ARE NARROW
    A hole two cells wide in a face only four cells wide removes everything
    holding the wall above it. Prefer tall narrow slits, or one full storey of
    glass - those are the two shapes that work.

3.4  ONE AXIS OF SYMMETRY AT MOST
    A building symmetric on both axes reads as a box whatever else it does.
    Offset the stair, the window, the roof box.

3.5  KEEP THE SILHOUETTE SIMPLE AND DISTINCT
    Nine buildings that share a palette need to differ in OUTLINE: legs, decks,
    a tall thin shaft, a low wide fort, a diagonal slide. If two levels have the
    same profile, one of them is wrong.


================================================================================
4. THE STANDARD TO MATCH - LEVEL 2, APPROVED
================================================================================

The Lifeguard Tower image is the reference for the whole mission. What it gets
right, and what every other image should copy:

    - four thin legs, clearly one cell each, clearly carrying the deck
    - the deck overhangs the legs by one cell, so the tower reads as built
      rather than extruded
    - the cabin sits inset on the deck, smaller than it
    - the flat roof overhangs the cabin again - two clean overhangs stacked
    - a raised box on the roof so the top is not a bare slab
    - a long stair with real treads on a solid stepped body, OFFSET to one side
    - two windows with a mullion between them, not one hole

The rendered game version of that image is attached as the style anchor. Your
images should look like buildings that would render like that.


================================================================================
5. THE NINE LEVELS
================================================================================

Format: grid in cells (wide x tall x deep), what to draw, and the support that
must be visible in the image.

LV1  BEACH HUT ROW                                    14 x 6 x 5
    Three tiny huts side by side, each on four short stilts, each with a
    DIFFERENT roof: one flat, one stepped, one peaked. Clear gaps of empty sand
    between them - they are three separate targets, not a terrace.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: four short stilts per hut, floors resting on their tops.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel beach huts, three small cube-block cabins side by
    side with different roofs - one flat, one stepped, one peaked - each raised
    on four short stilts, sandy brick tones, chunky cube blocks, plain white
    background"

LV2  LIFEGUARD TOWER                                  12 x 12 x 6     DONE
    Approved. Use it as the reference for the rest.

LV3  THE PIER                                         10 x 7 x 11
    A boardwalk running AWAY from the viewer into the distance: four post-and-
    beam frames one behind another carrying a plank deck, low railings, a small
    kiosk at the far end. The only level that uses depth as its subject - the
    image must show the recession clearly.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: two posts per frame, with deck planks sitting on the beam
    above each pair. Four frames, evenly spaced.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel wooden pier running away into the distance, four
    post-and-beam frames carrying a plank deck, low railings, small kiosk at the
    far end, brick-brown cubes, chunky blocks, plain white background"

LV4  BEACH BAR                                        12 x 7 x 7
    Open front - no front wall at all. A heavy flat roof carried by the back
    wall and just two slim posts at the front corners. Pale blue glass panels
    closing the two sides. A counter with small blocks lined up on it.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: the two front posts, roof resting directly on their tops.
    They should look barely adequate - that is the level's joke.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel beach bar, open front with a heavy flat roof
    carried on two thin posts, pale blue glass panels closing the two sides, a
    counter with small blocks on it, sandy brick and blue glass cubes, chunky
    blocks, plain white background"

LV5  SEAVIEW RESTAURANT                               12 x 9 x 7
    Two storeys plus a terrace. Solid brick ground floor with a wide entrance.
    The ENTIRE middle storey is glass - no brick corners, no mullions, a clean
    glass band all the way round. Brick roof terrace on top with small blocks
    and parasols.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: none needed - the glass storey IS the weak point and it
    must look like it. Do not draw columns through it.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel seaside restaurant, solid sandy brick ground floor,
    the entire middle storey made of pale blue glass with no columns, brick
    rooftop terrace with small blocks and parasols, chunky cube blocks, plain
    white background"

LV6  WATERSLIDE TOWER                                 14 x 11 x 7
    A brick stair tower with narrow slit windows, and a pale blue glass tube
    descending from the top in a stepped diagonal to a low pool rim. A small
    ticket kiosk stands separately beside it.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: THIS IS THE CRITICAL ONE. Thin brick props under the slide
    every two or three cells of its run, each standing on the ground, with the
    tube resting on top of them. A slide attached only to the tower's flank
    cannot be built.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel water slide tower, tall brick stair tower with
    narrow slit windows, a stepped diagonal pale blue glass tube descending from
    the top to a low pool rim, thin brick props standing on the ground holding
    the tube up along its whole run, small ticket kiosk to the side, chunky cube
    blocks, plain white background"

LV7  LIGHTHOUSE                                       9 x 14 x 6
    Grey concrete rock base. A tapering brick shaft that steps inward twice as
    it rises. Pale blue glass lantern room at the top with a grey gallery rail.
    A small brick keeper's cottage at the foot, standing separately.
    First appearance of concrete. Tallest thing in the mission.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: the shaft on its base. Keep the base SMALL - two or three
    rows - it is meant to be smashed by the falling tower, not shot.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel lighthouse, small grey concrete rock base, tapering
    sandy brick tower stepping inward twice as it rises, pale blue glass lantern
    room at the top with a grey gallery rail, small brick keeper's cottage at
    the foot, chunky cube blocks, plain white background"

LV8  SEA FORT                                         12 x 10 x 8
    A low grey concrete bastion on a stone plinth, with a CONTINUOUS horizontal
    pale blue glass slit running right around it. Inside the open courtyard,
    two small brick huts. Alternating grey caps along the top edge.
    The slit is the way in and must be unbroken - no concrete mullions.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: none - this is the one level solved by shooting through the
    opening rather than by cutting a leg.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel coastal fort, low grey concrete bastion on a stone
    plinth with a continuous horizontal pale blue glass slit running around it,
    open courtyard inside containing two small sandy brick huts, alternating
    grey caps along the top edge, chunky cube blocks, plain white background"

LV9  BOARDWALK RESORT  (BOSS)                         16 x 12 x 10
    Three buildings sharing one beach: the lifeguard tower on legs, the open
    beach bar, and a short pier stub on piles. A low grey breakwater along the
    front ties them together. Draw them CLOSE - close enough that the tower
    would fall onto the pier and the pier onto the bar - but with clear gaps of
    empty sand between them.
    VISIBLE SUPPORT: legs, posts and piles on all three, as in their own levels.
    Draw everything one size smaller than its own level - three buildings have
    to fit where one usually goes.
    PROMPT: "isometric voxel beach resort scene, a lifeguard tower on four thin
    legs, an open beach bar with a flat roof on posts and blue glass side
    panels, a short wooden pier on piles, a low grey breakwater along the front,
    sandy brick and blue glass and grey stone cubes, chunky blocks, plain white
    background"

6. WHAT TO SEND BACK
================================================================================

Per level, two things and nothing else:

    1. The image.
    2. Three lines of text:
         dimensions used:   e.g. "12 wide x 12 tall x 6 deep"
         visible support:   e.g. "four legs, deck resting on their tops"
         anything you were unsure fits the grid

If a building will not fit its grid, SAY SO instead of shrinking the bricks.
The grid can be argued about; the cell size cannot.

7. CHECKLIST BEFORE SENDING
================================================================================

    [ ] one brick = one cell; count the cubes along the top edge
    [ ] dimensions match the level's grid line in section 5
    [ ] only brick, glass and concrete; concrete only on LV7-9
    [ ] plain white background, isometric, same angle as the others
    [ ] every raised mass stands on a leg that is visible in the image
    [ ] nothing hangs off a side with nothing underneath
    [ ] openings are real holes, narrow, and you can see through them
    [ ] at most one axis of symmetry
    [ ] the silhouette is different from the other eight
