SMASH - MISSION 3 - SPACEPORT
Production brief: one image prompt and one JSON prompt per level
Project: Demolition Contracts (FierceTigerGameJam)     Revision 1 - 2026-08-31
================================================================================

WHO THIS IS FOR
    Two different AIs, and they must not do each other's job.

    THE IMAGE AI reads section 2 and its own prompt in section 5. It produces
    ONE picture per level. It does not produce JSON, wall groupings, entry
    counts or manifests - those are discarded on arrival.

    THE JSON AI reads section 3 and its own prompt in section 5. It produces
    ONE map file per level. It builds from the picture and the prompt together;
    where they disagree, the prompt wins, because the prompt is measured against
    the engine and the picture is not.

WHY THE SPLIT, STATED PLAINLY
    In Mission 2 the same AI did both and delivered nine maps that passed every
    automated check and were still wrong: huts twice as tall as they were wide, a
    lighthouse gallery that flared from six cells to twenty in a single row, a
    fort you could see straight through. None of that is visible to a validator.
    All of it is obvious in a render. Meanwhile its concept images were good.
    So: draw with the eye, build with the ruler.

WHAT IS ALREADY SETTLED AND MUST NOT BE RENEGOTIATED
    Theme, level order, grids and collapse stories come from
    SMASH_Mission2_3_Level_Blueprint section 4 and are reproduced here.
    Coating rules come from SMASH_WallCoating_Spec.
    Everything in sections 1 and 3 was read out of the engine source and the
    shipped config on 2026-08-31. It is not opinion and not up for discussion.


================================================================================
1. THE MISSION IN ONE PARAGRAPH
================================================================================

A planetary spaceport: landed ships, domes, gantries, station modules on
cradles. The engine has gravity and nothing may float, so every "space"
structure is GROUNDED - landed, docked, or standing on pedestal cradles. That
constraint is the mission's gameplay. Almost everything stands on legs, and legs
are for cutting.

Palette carries the theme with the same three materials as every other mission:

    CONCRETE grey   hull plate, armour, cradle stands, launch pads
    GLASS blue      domes, viewports, cockpit bands, solar panels
    BRICK sandy     painted cargo modules, scaffolds, crates

DIFFICULTY POSITION
    Mission 3 is the last mission and the hardest. Both brick AND concrete are
    coated from level 1 - no ramp-in. The player is expected to arrive with
    upgraded ammunition. This has a hard consequence the JSON AI must
    understand, spelled out in 3.6: concrete takes ZERO damage from Rock at
    either level, so any concrete in a level gates that level to the Cannon.

2. RULES FOR THE IMAGE AI
================================================================================

2.1  ONE CUBE IN YOUR IMAGE = ONE CELL OF THE GRID
    Everything is built from cubes 0.25 m on a side. There are no smaller
    pieces, ever. Read the grid line for your level - "14 wide x 8 tall x 7
    deep" means exactly 14 cubes across, 8 up, 7 back. Count the cubes along
    the top edge of your drawing. If the count does not match, redraw. Do not
    draw a hull as a smooth surface, a dome as a curve, or a rocket as a cone:
    draw them as stepped stacks of cubes, because that is what will be built.

2.2  EVERYTHING RAISED STANDS ON A VISIBLE LEG
    A rocket on legs, a shuttle on landing gear, a station core on cradles, a
    control tower on a stem - draw the support, and draw the mass RESTING on
    its top, not hovering a cell above it. Anything that appears to hang off a
    side with nothing underneath cannot be built: in the engine a body held only
    from the side is never released, so it hangs in the air forever and the
    level cannot be finished. Cantilevers - solar wings, umbilical arms, stub
    wings - are allowed but must overhang by ONE cell only, or carry a visible
    prop.

2.3  HOLLOW, NOT SOLID
    These are shells one cell thick with real openings. Draw doors, viewports
    and hangar mouths as holes you can see through, not as painted rectangles.

2.4  OPENINGS ARE NARROW, OR A WHOLE STOREY
    A two-cell hole in a four-cell face removes everything holding the wall
    above it. The two shapes that work are a tall narrow slit, and one full
    band of glass all the way round. Nothing in between.

2.5  PROPORTION - THE FAULT THAT KILLED FOUR MISSION 2 MAPS
    For a BUILDING (hangar, observatory drum, lab annex, biodome, station core):
    height must not exceed 1.5x the larger of width and depth.
    For a MAST (gantry, rocket, control tower stem): height may be up to 3x the
    footprint, but NO ROW MAY BE MORE THAN 6 CELLS WIDER THAN THE ROW BELOW IT,
    and no cell may sit more than one cell outside the footprint beneath it.
    Taper, do not flare. A head on a stalk is the single most common failure.

2.6  DENSITY
    A shell meant to read as solid must be at least 95 per cent of its
    perimeter. A shell meant to read as open needs ONE stated opening, not
    scattered gaps. "Some cells missing here and there" always reads as damage.

2.7  ONE AXIS OF SYMMETRY AT MOST
    Offset the door, the annex, the antenna. Symmetric on both axes reads as a
    box whatever else it does.

2.8  DISTINCT SILHOUETTES
    Nine buildings sharing one palette must differ in OUTLINE: a low barrel
    hangar, a ladder-like open frame, a fat legged rocket, a drum with a dome,
    two domes and a tube, a mushroom on a stem, a long low ship, a cylinder on
    cradles, and the whole port together. If two read the same, one is wrong.

2.9  PRESENTATION
    Isometric three-quarter, slightly from above, THE SAME ANGLE for all nine.
    Plain white or very light grey background. No ground plane, no sky, no
    scenery, no stars - the mission gets its space from the background art in
    the game, not from your drawing. The building alone.

2.10 WHAT TO SEND BACK, PER LEVEL
    The image, plus three lines:
        dimensions used:   "14 wide x 8 tall x 7 deep"
        visible support:   "four landing legs, fuselage resting on their tops"
        anything you were unsure fits the grid
    If a building will not fit its grid, SAY SO. The grid can be argued about;
    the cell size cannot.

3. RULES FOR THE JSON AI
================================================================================

These were read out of the engine source, not inferred. Breaking any of them
produces a map that validates and then misbehaves in play.

3.1  FILE SHAPE
    {
      "schemaVersion": 1,
      "id": "map_m3_00N_short_name",
      "grid": { "width": W, "height": H, "cellSize": 0.25, "layerDepth": 0.25 },
      "layers": [ { "level": 0, "blocks": [ ... ] }, ... ]
    }
    A layer is a DEPTH slice; "level" is its z. Inside a block, position.x runs
    across the width and position.y is height above the ground. Block ids follow
    "f{z}_c{x}_r{y}". cellSize is SPACING, not scale - never change it.

3.2  THE ONLY FOUR BLOCK TYPES
        brick_1x1      3 HP    1.2 kg
        brick_2x1      5 HP    2.4 kg   spans X; rotation 90 spans Y instead.
                                        IT CAN NEVER SPAN Z. If you need two
                                        cells of depth, that is two entries.
        glass_1x1      1 HP    0.6 kg
        concrete_1x1   6 HP    2.0 kg
    rotation must be 0, 90, 180 or 270. Anything else is rejected outright.

3.3  WALLS - THE RULE THAT BITES
    A wall_id groups blocks into ONE rigidbody. Its hit points are the SUM of
    its members' hit points. When it dies it bursts into its member blocks.

    A WALL IS COUNTED IN ENTRIES, NOT CELLS. The engine builds any wall with
    fewer than TWO ENTRIES as a plain loose block and logs a warning. So a wall
    of two cells written as a single brick_2x1 is not a wall at all - it
    silently becomes a loose block. Write those two cells as two brick_1x1.

    A wall must also be CONNECTED. The engine splits a disconnected wall_id into
    connected parts, and any part with fewer than two entries becomes loose. Do
    not group four corner legs under one wall_id: that is one body whose
    collider spans the whole footprint, i.e. a solid slab where you drew air.

    Size: hard bounds 2-30 cells, comfortable 4-24. One wall per face per band
    is the shape that works - see how LV8 of Mission 2 is grouped.

    A wall may not span two separate structures. Prefix every wall_id with its
    structure ("shuttle_leg_fl", "gantry_frame_lo", "core_cradle_left") and
    never let a prefix bridge a gap.

3.4  SUPPORT - THE FAULT THAT MAKES A LEVEL UNFINISHABLE
    The cascade releases a body when nothing overlaps it from below in BOTH x
    and z. A body held only from the SIDE is never released: it hangs in the air
    for the rest of the run and 100 per cent clear becomes impossible.
    Therefore: every body must have something under part of its own footprint,
    and no body may sit more than one cell outside the footprint beneath it.
    Cantilevers get a prop on the ground every two or three cells of their run.

3.5  COATING
    An armoured wall is the default. Absence of a flag means armoured; write
        "wall": { "wall_id": "x", "bare": true }
    only to opt a wall OUT of its coating. An armoured wall takes the bullet's
    wallDamage; a bare one takes the much larger blockDamage.
    MISSION 3: everything is coated, brick and concrete alike, from level 1.
    Use "bare" only where a specific level note below says so.

3.6  THE DAMAGE TABLE - READ THIS BEFORE CHOOSING A MATERIAL
                        glass          brick          concrete
                     block / wall   block / wall    block / wall
        Rock   Lv1       5 / 5          3 / 1           0 / 0
        Rock   Lv2       6 / 6          4 / 3           0 / 0
        Cannon Lv1       8 / 8          6 / 5           4 / 2
        Cannon Lv2      10 / 10         8 / 7           7 / 5

    CONCRETE IS IMMUNE TO ROCK. Not slow - zero. A single concrete wall in the
    load path gates the whole level to the Cannon. That is intended for Mission
    3, but it means concrete must never be the ONLY way to reach 50 per cent.
    GLASS HAS NO COATING ADVANTAGE - its block and wall numbers are identical at
    every level, which is why glass is always the cheap target.

3.7  WHAT WINNING MEANS
    Clear per cent = destroyed blocks / blocks actually placed. Pass at
    requiredClearPercent (0.5 across the campaign today); full clear at 1.0.
    A block breaks when it lands at 1.5 m/s or faster, so TOPPLING CLEARS MASS -
    a structure that falls from three rows up shatters on the pad. This is why
    the mission is built on legs: the player spends shots on supports, not on
    grinding walls.

    DO THE SHOT ARITHMETIC BEFORE YOU DELIVER. Worked example, Mission 2 LV1:
    twelve legs of two brick cells = 6 HP each; Cannon Lv1 does 5 wall damage,
    so two shots a leg; two legs felled per hut, three huts, twelve shots - and
    the level carries twelve ammunition. State this arithmetic in your delivery
    note for every level.

3.8  BUDGETS
    Entries: follow the per-level target below, plus or minus 15 per cent.
    Loose blocks (no wall_id): 150 maximum on the opening frame, and prefer far
    fewer - they are individual rigidbodies.
    Grid: 16 wide, 14 tall, 12 layers deep are the ceilings. Portrait screen:
    anything wider than 16 renders small.
    Centring is automatic. The engine now centres a map on the cells that are
    actually filled, not on the declared grid width, so you do NOT need to pad a
    grid symmetrically. Declare the grid that fits your building.

3.9  SELF-CHECK BEFORE DELIVERING - ALL SIX, EVERY LEVEL
    [ ] no wall under 2 entries, none over 30 cells, none disconnected
    [ ] no body more than one cell outside the footprint below it
    [ ] every structure's prefix is its own; no wall bridges a gap
    [ ] proportion rule of 2.5 holds for every named structure
    [ ] shot arithmetic reaches 50 per cent inside the ammunition, with the
        bullet the level assumes
    [ ] RENDER THE MAP AND LOOK AT IT. Four of Mission 2's nine maps passed
        every automated check and were still wrong. Rendering catches it in one
        glance.

4. THE STANDARD TO MATCH
================================================================================

Approved and shipped, use these as the reference for density and grouping:

    Mission 2 LV2  lifeguard tower    102 entries   15 walls   8x11x6
        Four one-cell legs, deck overhanging them by a cell, cabin inset on the
        deck, roof overhanging the cabin again, a raised box on the roof, an
        offset stair. Two clean overhangs stacked. This is the house style.

    Mission 2 LV8  sea fort           338 entries   28 walls   12x10x8
        How to group a shell: one wall per face per band. Front and back split
        in half because a full face across three rows is 36 cells, past the size
        where a wall still behaves like one slab. Six walls per band, twelve in
        all, plus four for the glass slit and four for the plinth.

    Mission 2 LV1  beach hut row      150 entries   21 walls   16x6x3
        How to build legs: two cells each, own wall, four per hut, floor resting
        on their tops. One cell would be demoted to a loose block and could not
        be cut as a wall.

    Mission 2 LV9  boardwalk resort   259 entries   43 walls   16x9x10
        How to make several structures share a map: strict prefixes, clear sand
        between them, and exactly one deliberate interaction - the pier deck
        reaches one cell over the bar's roof so felling one lands on the other.

LV1  CARGO HANGAR                      grid 14 x 8 x 7   ~140 entries   ammo 14
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MATERIALS  brick shell, brick crates. No concrete, no glass.
COATING    brick coated. Crates bare - see the JSON prompt.
LESSON     the arch IS the building. Chip a haunch and the roof ring drops into
           the crate stack.

IMAGE PROMPT
    "Isometric voxel spaceport cargo hangar built from chunky sandy-brick cubes,
    drawn three-quarter view slightly from above on a plain white background.
    A low wide barrel-profile shed, 14 cubes across, 8 cubes tall, 7 cubes deep,
    wider than it is tall. Its roof is a STEPPED arch - a staircase of cubes
    rising from each side wall to a flat crown three cubes wide, never a smooth
    curve. The front face is almost entirely one huge rectangular door opening
    you can see straight through, four cubes wide and four cubes tall, offset to
    the left of centre rather than centred, with a narrow column of brick left
    standing on each side. Inside, visible through the opening, a loose stack of
    small brick crate cubes piled two and three high, untidy, not aligned to the
    walls. The side walls are one cube thick with one tall narrow slit window
    each. Nothing floats: the arch springs from the tops of the side walls and
    every crate rests on the floor or on another crate. Cube blocks with visible
    individual faces, warm sandy red-brown, no wood grain, no metal, no
    lettering, no ground plane, no sky."

JSON PROMPT
    Build map_m3_001_cargo_hangar, grid 14 x 8 x 7.
    Shell: side walls at x0-1 and x12-13, full depth z0-6, rows y0-y4. Group
    each side as two walls of about 15 cells - lower band y0-y2, upper y3-y4.
    Arch: rows y5, y6, y7 stepping inward - y5 spans x1-12, y6 spans x2-11, y7
    spans x4-9 as the flat crown. Each arch row is one wall per half, split at
    the centre line, so a haunch can be taken off without the whole roof going
    at once. No row may be more than 6 cells narrower than the row below.
    Door: remove x4-x7 from the front face at z0 for rows y0-y3. Leave x2-x3 and
    x8-x11 standing as the door jambs, each its own wall of 8 cells. Do NOT
    centre the opening.
    Back wall z6 is solid across x2-11, rows y0-y4, in two walls.
    Crates: 18 to 24 brick_1x1 with NO wall_id, sitting on the floor at y0 and
    y1 inside the shed, in a ragged pile. Mark nothing bare except these - they
    have no wall so the flag does not apply to them.
    Support: every arch row must overlap the row beneath it in both x and z.
    Check: shell rows at least 95 per cent complete apart from the one door.
    Shot arithmetic to state: a haunch wall of 15 brick cells is 45 HP; Cannon
    Lv1 does 5 wall damage, so 9 shots - too many. Split the haunch into three
    walls of 5 cells (15 HP, 3 shots) so the first collapse is reachable inside
    14 ammunition.

    Shot arithmetic: a 12-cell brick column is 36 HP, Cannon Lv1 5 per shot = 8
    shots. That is too many for a domino to be the fun answer. Split each column
    into a lower wall of 5 cells and an upper of 7, so cutting the lower one
    (15 HP, 3 shots) drops the tower.
